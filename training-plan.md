## Testing Protocol

Three tests across the block. Each one updates your VDOT and recalibrates pacing for the next phase.

### Test 1 — 10k TT (Week 2, Wednesday May 20)

**Why 10k:**
 The 10k is run right at or just above LT2 — the exact zone you're 
training most. It gives cleaner zone calibration than a 5k (which is 
partly above VO2max) and anchors your T-pace sessions directly.

**Protocol:**

- WU: 15 min easy (5:15-5:30/km) + 4 × 80m strides with full walk recovery
- Km 1-2: Deliberately conservative — the easiest km of the race should be km 1
- Km 3-8: Settle into race rhythm — controlled discomfort, even splits
- Km 9-10: Empty the tank; final 500m should feel maximal
- CD: 10 min easy

**Record:** Total time, avg pace, avg HR, max HR, 5k split / second 5k split (pacing evenness indicator)

**Interpret:** Input into vdoto2.com → get VDOT + all training zones → use these immediately

**Expected range (given 17:50 twisty 5k):** ~36:40-37:30

## Weekly Structure

| Day | Role | Loic? | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mon** | Easy + strides | Yes | Medium |
| **Tue** | Quality session (see phase) | No | **High** |
| **Wed** | Primary quality session | No | **Non-negotiable** |
| **Thu** | Compulsory rest | — | — |
| **Fri** | Easy | Yes | Low |
| **Sat** | Long run | Yes (easy only) | High |
| **Sun** | Rest | — | — |

**On the Tuesday-Wednesday back-to-back:**
 This is a known trade-off. Seiler is explicit that two quality sessions
 is the maximum most trained athletes can sustain, and that the recovery
 cost is real. Having them on consecutive days is not ideal but many 
runners use this layout successfully. It works if and only if:

1. Monday is **genuinely easy** — no creeping above 5:10/km
2. Tuesday sessions are kept **SHORT** (16-32 min of actual work) and are never taken to absolute max
3. You accept that sometimes Wednesday will start stiff, and warm-up properly (12-15 min minimum before any threshold effort)
4. If Wednesday ever feels truly terrible after 12 min WU, convert it to 30 min easy and add the session the following week

If this pattern is consistently problematic, the fix is simple: **move I-pace to Monday**
 (replace strides with the full interval session), let Tuesday be easy, 
and keep Wednesday as T-pace. This gives a full day of separation.

## Guardrails

**Intensity distribution:**
 Per Seiler's framework, total training time spent above LT1 should stay
 at roughly 20% or below. With two quality sessions of ~45-55 min each 
(WU+main+CD), plus long run inserts in Phase 4, that accounts for 
approximately 90-110 min of above-LT1 work against ~5-6 hr of total easy
 work — you're in the right range as long as the easy runs are genuinely
 easy.

**Volume escalation:** Maximum 10% increase on
 build weeks. If a week runs short (illness, travel, life), don't try to
 make up the km the following week — just continue from where you are.

**Red flags — drop quality sessions that week:**

- Calf or Achilles tightness persisting >48-72 hours
- Resting HR elevated >5-7 bpm above normal on waking
- Easy pace feels hard at normal easy HR
- Back-to-back poor sleep nights going into a quality session

**If a week goes wrong:** Hierarchy of what to protect:

1. Wednesday quality (anchor session — never skip)
2. Saturday long run
3. Tuesday quality
4. Monday/Friday easy runs (these are expendable individually)

**Heat adjustment (important for summer training):**
 Above ~20°C, slow all paces by 5-15 sec/km. Above 25°C, abandon pace 
targets entirely and run by perceived effort. I-pace in a heatwave 
becomes "hard controlled 4-5 min rep effort" not a specific number.

**When to update VDOT zones:**
 After Test 1 (Week 2), Test 2 (Week 9), and Test 3 (Week 17). Not after
 every good or bad session. The research-based recommendation is every 
4-6 weeks or after a meaningful performance shift (≥30s on a 5k, ≥60s on
 a 10k). Frequent recalibration undermines training continuity.
